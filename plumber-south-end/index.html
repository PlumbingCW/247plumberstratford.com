Planning guide.

What the planning documents say about the South End.

A 2023 UConn CIRCA workshop on a resilient South End studied the South End neighborhood together with the Lordship Commercial and Industrial District in Stratford. The slides name Lordship Boulevard, Sikorsky Memorial Airport and the Housatonic River as reference points, and they do not define exact boundaries. We describe the area the same way.

The materials note that the town's earlier resilience plan identified frequent flooding pathways during storms with a greater than 1 percent annual chance, such as Hurricane Sandy. They list sea level rise and inland and coastal flooding among the hazards, and they name storm water flooding as a priority. The town retained a consultant to begin an engineering study.

What you control and what the town is studying.

Public projects take years. Your basement needs protection this season, so start with what you control.

Things you can do at home.

  • Test the sump pump before every storm season.
  • Ask about a battery backup for the pump.
  • Keep floor drains clear.
  • Lift stored items off the basement floor.
  • Know where the main shutoff is.

What public projects are studying.

  • A storm water engineering study retained by the town.
  • Flood improvements at the Water Pollution Control Facility.
  • The Bruce Brook Culvert.
  • Protection along the Housatonic River.

Storm water or sewage backing up in the South End.

If water comes up through a floor drain or a toilet during heavy rain, stop using water in the house, keep everyone away from the wet area and call. Do not wade through standing water near electrical outlets or a panel.

A plumber can tell whether the cause is a blocked line, a failed pump or a surge that a one-way valve might help with. See sewer line cleaning and sump pump repair.

A four-step storm checklist for a South End basement.

See basement waterproofing for permanent options.

Step 1.

Pump test.

Fill the pit with a bucket of water and watch the pump run and shut off.

Step 2.

Backup power.

Ask about a battery backup, since storms can cut the power when the pump matters most.

Step 3.

Drain check.

Make sure the floor drain flows freely and the cleanout cap is accessible.

Step 4.

Shutoff check.

Confirm you can reach and turn the main valve.

Local note.

Sewer, storm water and digging.

Ask Stratford Water Pollution Control which sewer serves your address and whether storm water and sanitary lines are separate at your property. Call 811 before any digging. A plumber is not the right contact for flood zones or insurance, so ask the town for flood zone information.
