Leak detection in Stratford, CT, before the wall opens.

Leak detection is the process of locating a water leak without tearing out finished surfaces, using sound, heat, pressure and flow readings to narrow the leak to a few feet. The aim is to open one small access point instead of a whole wall or floor.

If water is stained on a ceiling, a wall is damp or the meter keeps spinning with everything off, call the 24/7 line. Call to ask about timing. Meanwhile close the main shutoff, avoid electrical outlets near wet areas and move valuables out from under the stain.

Act now.

When a hidden leak is an emergency.

Active water coming through a ceiling, a warm spot on a floor, or a meter that races after you close every tap are all urgent signs. Water that reaches a light fixture or an outlet adds an electrical danger, so switch off power to the room at the panel if you can do it safely.

After hours, the 24/7 line works the same way. Tell the dispatcher where the water shows and whether the main can be shut. That lets the plumber arrive with the right locator and a plan for a quick stop.

The meter test: is it really a leak.

Water utilities bill by meter reading, so the meter is your first witness. Shut off every tap and appliance, then watch the small triangle or dial on the meter for a minute. If it moves, water is flowing somewhere. If the number only moves when the ice maker or irrigation runs, it is not a leak.

A leak between the meter and the house is the owner's to repair in most utility setups. Ask your water utility to confirm where its responsibility ends. For Stratford addresses served by Aquarion, that confirmation is a single call to the utility.

Hidden leaks.

Where leaks hide, and how we find them.

Ceiling and wall stains.

Thermal imaging shows moisture patterns, and a moisture meter confirms them before we open anything.

Slab and under-floor leaks.

Listening gear picks up the sound of pressurized water escaping, which narrows a slab leak to a short run of pipe.

Crawlspace and under-house runs.

We inspect pipe hangers, joints and drains directly where access allows, with a pressure test to confirm.

Buried service lines.

A drop in pressure test and acoustic listening along the yard trace a leak between meter and house.

Irrigation lines.

Sprinkler zones are tested separately, since a stuck valve or split lateral can mimic a house leak.

Pool and spa plumbing.

We can isolate and pressure-test supply lines to a pool, and refer you to a pool specialist for shell leaks.

If a street near you is under main work, a sudden pressure change may come from the utility rather than your pipes, so check with a meter test first.
