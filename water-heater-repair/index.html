Water heater repair in Stratford, CT, when the hot water quits.

Water heater repair means finding the failed part in a tank or tankless unit, such as a heating element, thermostat, gas valve, igniter or relief valve, and replacing it so the heater works safely again. Many failures are part-level fixes, not full replacements.

The 24/7 line is open every night and weekend. While you wait, turn the heater's power or gas control to off if water is leaking, shut the cold supply valve on top of the tank, and keep towels away from the floor drain path.

Act now.

When a water heater problem is an emergency.

Call right away for water pooling around the base, a hissing or screaming relief valve, a burning smell, scorched paint near the burner or any smell of gas. A leaking tank can flood a finished basement in an afternoon, and a stuck relief valve is a pressure risk.

If you smell gas, leave the house first, then call 911 and your gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches or search for the leak yourself. After the house is safe, call us for the repair.

Symptoms.

What your symptom usually means.

No hot water at all.

On an electric tank, a failed element or tripped thermostat is common. On gas, look for a pilot or igniter that will not light.

Lukewarm water that runs out fast.

A burned-out lower element or a thick layer of sediment cuts how much water the tank can heat.

Popping or rumbling.

Sediment at the bottom traps steam bubbles. The sound often comes before an element failure or a leak.

Rust-colored hot water.

If only the hot side is rusty, the tank lining or anode rod is likely worn. Rust on both sides points to the house pipes.

Water at the base of the tank.

A loose fitting or relief valve discharge can be fixed. A weeping tank seam cannot, and the unit must be replaced.

Pilot keeps going out.

A worn thermocouple, a dirty burner or a venting draft problem can all blow out a flame.

Gas, electric and hybrid heaters: what we repair.

Gas heaters get thermocouple, gas control valve, igniter and venting repairs, plus a combustion safety check before we leave. Electric heaters get element, thermostat and wiring work, tested with a meter rather than guessed at.

Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor, fan and condensate drain to the checklist, and their service needs a plumber comfortable with both refrigeration and water. We will say so up front if a unit needs the manufacturer's technician, and we never open a sealed refrigerant circuit ourselves.

Repair or replace: a quick decision guide.

If the answer is replacement, our water heater replacement page explains what the swap involves, and a unit that is simply the wrong size for the household may be a better fit as a tankless water heater.

What affects the cost of a water heater repair.

Price depends on the part and on how easy the unit is to reach. We quote before we start.

The failed part.A thermostat, element, igniter or gas valve each carry different parts and labor.
Fuel type.Gas units need venting and combustion checks that add steps electric units skip.
Unit age and model.Older models can have scarce parts. If a part is unavailable, replacement may be the only route.
Location.A heater in a tight closet or attic takes longer to service than one in an open basement.
Timing.Evening and weekend visits are scheduled the same way as daytime visits, with the dispatcher confirming the window.

A sudden burst from the heater may be covered by homeowners insurance, while wear and slow leaks usually are not. Photograph any water damage and keep the failed parts.

Local note.

Water heaters and Stratford's water.

Stratford is on Aquarion Water Company's Eastern Fairfield County system, and the 2025 water quality report lists average hardness at 3 grains per gallon with a range of 2 to 5. That is on the soft side, so scale builds on elements more slowly than it does in hard-water areas. Sediment still settles in any tank, so a yearly flush is still worth doing.

Permit rules vary, so ask the Stratford building department whether a heater replacement in your house needs a permit. Repairs to elements and thermostats usually do not change the installation, but your plumber can tell you when a job crosses that line.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission urges users to lower their water heaters to 120 degrees Fahrenheit to reduce the risk of tap water scalds, so ask the plumber to set the thermostat with that guideline in mind.
