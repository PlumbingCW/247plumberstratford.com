Water heater installation in Stratford, CT, planned the right way.

Water heater installation is more than setting a tank on the floor. It includes sizing the unit, running the supply and gas or electrical connections, venting any combustion gases, adding a relief valve with a safe discharge line, and testing everything under load.

This page is for planned installs, for example a new home, an upgrade or a remodel. If your current heater is leaking today, go straight to our water heater replacement page, which is built for urgent calls.

Scope.

What goes into a proper installation.

A good installation looks boring when it is done, with neat pipe runs, labeled valves and no drips.

Sizing.

Capacity and recovery rate are matched to the number of bathrooms, fixtures and people.

Venting.

Gas units need correctly sized and pitched venting, and the combustion air supply must be adequate.

Relief valve and discharge.

A temperature and pressure relief valve needs a full-size discharge pipe that ends in a safe place.

Expansion control.

A closed water system can need an expansion tank so rising pressure has somewhere to go.

Shutoffs and unions.

Isolation valves and unions make the next service visit faster and cleaner.

Drain pan and leak protection.

In finished spaces we recommend a pan and a drain path so a future leak stays contained.

Which type fits your home.

We lay out the real costs of each path, including the unglamorous parts like venting and wiring. For a deeper look at the on-demand option, see tankless water heater service.

Type.Good fit when.Watch for.

Permits, codes and who checks the work.

Plumbing work in Connecticut falls under the state building code, and the local building office enforces it. Permit rules vary by town and by job, so ask the Stratford building department what your installation needs. The Office of the Building Official lists an online permitting portal for applications.

You can also confirm that any plumber you hire holds an active Connecticut license through the state's eLicense lookup, run by the Department of Consumer Protection. Search by name or business and check that the status reads active before work begins.

What shapes the price of an installation.

Because every house differs, we give a clear price after seeing the space.

Unit type.A tank, tankless unit and hybrid heater sit at very different price points.
Venting changes.Moving from an atmospheric vent to a sealed or power vent adds materials and labor.
Gas and electrical work.A larger gas line or a new circuit can be the biggest hidden item.
Location and access.Attics, crawlspaces and finished closets slow the job.

Local note.

Installing in a Stratford home.

Stratford's Aquarion supply reports average hardness of 3 grains per gallon in the 2025 Eastern Fairfield County report, so scale is a smaller issue than in much of the country. That helps a tankless unit, which scales in its heat exchanger over time, though a yearly descale is still sensible.

Shoreline properties see humid summers and salt air. For an outdoor-vented or garage-mounted unit, ask the plumber about corrosion-resistant fittings and a raised base.

According to the International Code Council, Connecticut's state building codes are based on the 2021 International Codes, so ask the Stratford building department which edition applies to your project.
