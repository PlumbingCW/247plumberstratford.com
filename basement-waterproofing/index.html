Basement waterproofing in Stratford, CT.

Basement waterproofing is a set of methods for keeping groundwater out of a below-grade space, from managing roof and yard drainage to installing interior drains and a sump pump. The right method depends on how water gets in.

This is a planned service. If your basement is flooding now, call the 24/7 line and a plumber can address the emergency, then we can plan the permanent fix. Move items off the floor, shut off power to wet outlets and do not run a vacuum in standing water.

Water paths.

Where basement water comes from.

Cracks in walls or floor.

Hydrostatic pressure forces water through cracks and cold joints, especially after heavy rain.

Floor to wall joint.

The joint where slab meets wall is a common seepage line.

Window wells.

Clogged or poorly drained window wells overflow into the basement.

Surface grading and gutters.

Water sloping toward the foundation or discharging next to it saturates the soil.

High groundwater.

A rising water table pushes water through the slab from below.

Plumbing leaks.

A leaking pipe can look like groundwater. A leak detection check rules it in or out.

Waterproofing methods compared.

Interior drains usually pair with a sump pump, and we check the whole system for sense before suggesting it.

Crawlspaces and partial basements.

Many New England homes have a crawlspace or a half basement. A wet crawlspace can damage insulation, rot floor framing and feed mold. Fixes include a drain, a sump, a sealed vapor barrier and improved grading.

We describe what we see plainly, including when the problem is a plumbing leak, a missing gutter or a downspout that empties at the foundation. Not every wet basement needs a major system.

Local note.

Wet basements near the shore and river.

Stratford is bordered by Long Island Sound on the south and the Housatonic River on the east. Low-lying areas near the water can have higher groundwater, and a basement there deals with more water pressure than one on higher ground.

Before you hire anyone for exterior work, call 811 for utility locates and ask the Stratford building department whether permits apply. Permit rules vary, so check for your property and your planned work.

The national 811 number connects you to the utility locating service, and a locate request before digging is the standard first step for any buried line work. According to the International Code Council, Connecticut's state building codes are based on the 2021 International Codes, so ask the Stratford building department which edition applies to your project.
