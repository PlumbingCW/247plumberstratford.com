Causes of Clogged Drains in Stratford, CT Homes, and What Fixes Them

Almost every clog has a cause you can name. Knowing whether it is grease, hair, wipes or roots tells you whether a plunger will do or a plumber is needed.

One drain or the whole house.

The first question is how many fixtures are affected. If only one sink or tub drains slowly, the clog sits in that fixture or its branch line. If several fixtures slow down together or a toilet gurgles when the washer drains, the blockage is deeper, in the main line.

That difference decides what you try. A branch clog can often be cleared with a plunger or a small cable. A main line clog needs a longer cable or hydro jetting, and a camera helps find what is causing it.

Where clogs begin.

The usual causes, by room.

Kitchen: grease and food.

Fat cools inside the pipe and forms a waxy sleeve that traps rice, coffee grounds and food bits.

Bathroom sink: hair and soap.

Hair knits with soap scum and sticks to the pop-up stopper and trap.

Shower and tub: hair and residue.

Long hair and body wash residue build up just below the drain.

Toilet: too much paper or wipes.

Wipes sold as flushable do not break down like paper and catch in bends.

Laundry: lint and detergent.

Lint from the washer sticks to the inside of the standpipe.

Main line: roots and old pipe.

Roots enter at joints, and cracked or sagging pipe holds water and waste.

Why chemical cleaners are a poor first move.

Liquid drain openers are tempting, but they can sit in standing water, eat at old pipe and splash back on whoever opens the drain next. If a clog does not clear, the plumber then has to work around a harsh chemical.

A plunger, a drain snake or a baking soda and vinegar flush for a light film is safer. If none of these works, the clog is likely deeper than a home remedy can reach.

How a small clog becomes a big one.

Clogs rarely appear overnight. A thin film of grease or soap narrows the pipe a little each week, and then one handful of food scraps or a clump of hair closes it. The slow drain you ignored for a month was the warning, and clearing it early is simpler than dealing with a full blockage.

Pay attention to sounds and smells too. Gurgling means air is being pulled past a partial blockage. A musty smell from a drain points to residue, and a sewage smell points to a dry trap or a deeper problem. Each of these is a sign to act before water starts rising.

Local note.

Stratford note: trees, older homes and the sewer.

Stratford has many older streets with mature trees, and roots seeking moisture are a common cause of repeat main line clogs. If you are unsure whether your street has public sewer, ask the Stratford Water Pollution Control office. For work near the street, call 811 before any digging.

The same causes show up in nearby Bridgeport and Trumbull, where older cast iron laterals and mature trees are common too.

The national 811 number connects you to the utility locating service, and a locate request before digging is the standard first step for any buried line work.

Habits that prevent most clogs.

Scrape plates into the trash rather than the sink, and keep grease in a can. Use a hair catcher in showers. Flush only toilet paper. Run hot water after dishes, and have the main line cleaned if you notice slowing across the house.

If your drains clog more than once a year, ask for a camera inspection. Our sewer camera inspection shows exactly what is happening inside the pipe.
