What to tell the dispatcher.

A few details speed up the call and help the plumber bring the right parts. Have these ready if you can.

Your address and ZIP.

Include the unit number or any gate or access detail.

What you see.

Water spreading, a slow drain, no hot water or a smell all point to different causes.

What you have done.

Say whether the main is off and whether any chemicals went down a drain.

Who can let us in.

Tell us if a tenant, neighbor or manager will meet the plumber.

Call now or schedule a visit.

Call right away for burst pipes, flooding, sewage backups, leaking water heaters and heating failures in cold weather. Call to book a visit for slow drains, dripping faucets, running toilets and planned installs.

If you are not sure which applies, call. The dispatcher will tell you honestly whether it needs help tonight. You can also browse our <a href="/services/">services</a> to see what each repair involves.

Hours and response.

The phone line is open 24 hours a day, every day of the year. Weather, traffic and the number of open calls shape how quickly a plumber can reach you, so the dispatcher gives an honest window instead of a promise. During a major storm, calls are handled in order of urgency, and active flooding and sewage backups come first.

If you can wait until the next day, say so. Scheduled visits are easier to plan and leave emergency plumbers free for the calls that cannot wait.

Commercial and multi-unit properties.

Property managers and small business owners can use the same line. Tell the dispatcher it is a commercial or multi-unit property, who holds the keys and whether tenants are affected. See our commercial plumbing page for the services we cover.

Phone or email.

The phone is the fastest route for anything urgent, because a dispatcher can ask questions and dispatch at once. Email suits planned work, such as a quote for a water heater installation or a remodel walk-through. Include your address, a short description and a good time to call.

If you email about an emergency, call as well. Email is not monitored the way the phone line is.

Before the plumber arrives.

Know where your main water shutoff is and clear a path to it. Move valuables off wet floors and keep children and pets away from standing water. If the problem is at a sink or toilet, avoid running more water. If a gas smell is present, leave the house and call 911 and the gas utility from outside.
