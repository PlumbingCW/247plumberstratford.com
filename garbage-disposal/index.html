Garbage disposal repair and installation in Stratford, CT.

A garbage disposal grinds food waste under the sink so it can wash into the drain. When it jams, hums without spinning, leaks from the body or stops working entirely, the cause is usually a stuck impeller, a failed seal or a tripped reset.

If the sink is backing up or water is leaking under the cabinet, switch off the disposal at the wall, close the shutoff valves and call the 24/7 line. Call to ask about timing. Never put a hand inside the disposal, even when it is off.

Act now.

When a disposal problem is an emergency.

A leaking disposal can soak the cabinet floor and the cabinet beneath it. A disposal that is humming without turning can overheat. And a sink full of water that will not drain affects the whole kitchen.

Turn off the power at the switch or breaker, not just the wall button. Tell the dispatcher whether the disposal is jammed, leaking or dead, and what you last put through it.

Common failures.

Why a disposal stops working.

Jammed impeller.

A fork, bone, fruit pit or other hard item locks the grinding plate and trips the reset.

Humming with no spin.

The motor is trying to turn against a jam or has a failed start capacitor.

Leak from the bottom.

The housing seal has failed and the unit must be replaced.

Leak at the top or sides.

A loose flange, worn gasket or failed drain connection can be tightened or replaced.

No power.

A tripped breaker, a dead switch or a failed motor.

Slow draining sink.

Buildup in the trap arm or the line past the disposal.

Repair or replace: what usually makes sense.

A jam can often be cleared in minutes. A leak from the body, a seized motor or a unit past about eight to ten years usually means replacement. Because the repair parts and labor can approach the cost of a new unit, we tell you both numbers.

If the sink drain is slow even with the disposal clear, the clog may sit downstream. See our drain cleaning page for how kitchen lines are cleared. If a leak is coming from the sink faucet or supply line instead, our faucet and sink repair page can help.

Local note.

Disposals and Stratford kitchens.

Kitchens in Stratford homes may have disposals on septic or on the town sewer. A home on septic does best with a lighter use of the disposal, and a home on the town sewer depends on the line staying clear of grease.

Ask the Stratford Water Pollution Control office about any town rules on food waste and grease if you are unsure. Grease is the main cause of kitchen line blockages whatever the setup.

According to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, the eLicense lookup shows whether a contractor's license is active, and that matters most when wiring is involved.
