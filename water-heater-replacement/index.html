Water heater replacement in Stratford, CT.

Water heater replacement is the removal of a failed or end-of-life heater and the installation of a new one, including the water, gas or electric connections, venting and safety valves. In most Stratford homes it is a one-visit job.

If your tank is leaking now, call the 24/7 line. Call to ask about timing. Until the plumber arrives, switch off the power or gas supply, close the cold valve over the tank and move stored items out of the water's path.

Act now.

When a failed water heater is an emergency.

A tank that leaks from its seam will not stop leaking, and the amount of water can overwhelm a floor drain or soak drywall and boxes. No hot water in January is also a real emergency for a household with children or an older resident.

After hours, call the same number. Tell the dispatcher the tank size on the label, the fuel type and where the heater sits, because that lets us bring a matching unit and the right fittings.

Decision signs.

Signs it is time to replace, not repair.

Leak from the tank itself.

Water seeping from the seams or the bottom shell means the steel has failed.

Age past about 10 to 12 years.

Most tanks reach the end of their working life in this range, even without obvious symptoms.

Rusty hot water.

Rust that comes only from hot taps suggests the interior lining is breaking down.

Repeated repairs in two years.

A second or third service call on the same tank is a sign the parts are failing in sequence.

A unit that no longer keeps up.

Added bathrooms or family members can outgrow a 40-gallon tank.

Rising energy bills.

An old burner or heavy sediment layer wastes fuel to produce the same hot water.

Choosing the replacement: size, fuel and efficiency.

Match the capacity to household use. A common rule of thumb is about 40 gallons for one or two people and 50 or more for larger families, but showering habits and fixtures change that. We size by your actual use, not by the label on the old tank.

Fuel is usually kept the same because switching between gas and electric means new venting, gas piping or wiring. If you want to change, we will explain what the added work involves before you commit.

Standard tank or an upgrade.

Both are common in Fairfield County homes. If you are curious about the upgrade path, our tankless water heater page lays out the trade-offs in detail.

Standard tank.

  • Lower purchase and installation cost.
  • Drops into the existing connections in most homes.
  • Holds a limited volume of hot water.
  • Typical life of 8 to 12 years.

Tankless or hybrid.

  • Hot water on demand with no standby tank loss.
  • Needs added gas, venting or electrical work.
  • Higher upfront cost, lower running cost.
  • Longer service life when maintained.

What replacement day looks like.

1.

Confirm the unit.

We verify tank size, fuel, venting and any permit needs before we start.

2.

Drain and remove.

The old tank is drained, disconnected and hauled out of the home.

3.

Install.

The new heater is set, connected, vented and fitted with a relief valve and discharge line.

4.

Test.

We fill, fire up and check for leaks, temperature and safe combustion if gas.

What drives the price of a replacement.

You get a clear price before work starts, and these are the main variables.

Tank size and efficiency.A larger or higher-efficiency tank costs more to buy.
Fuel and venting.Gas units need proper venting, and a change of fuel adds work.
Code upgrades.Expansion tanks, relief valve discharge piping and similar fittings may be required on older installs.
Access.A heater in an attic or tight closet takes longer to remove and install.
Old unit removal.Hauling a full-size tank is a physical job that we plan for.

Insurance may help when a sudden leak damages the home. It rarely pays for the heater itself when the tank wore out. Keep the failed tank until your adjuster says otherwise.

Local note.

Replacement in a Stratford home.

Connecticut homes often tuck the heater in a basement or garage beside the boiler or furnace, and Stratford is no different. If yours shares a room with a boiler, tell us so the venting and clearances are checked together.

Ask the Stratford building department whether your replacement needs a permit, since permit rules vary by job. The Office of the Building Official runs an online permitting portal, and a plumber can tell you what information to have ready.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission urges users to lower their water heaters to 120 degrees Fahrenheit to reduce the risk of tap water scalds, so ask the plumber to set the thermostat with that guideline in mind. According to the International Code Council, Connecticut's state building codes are based on the 2021 International Codes, so ask the Stratford building department which edition applies to your project.

Condominium owners can read how association rules affect a replacement in our Oronoque Village guide.
