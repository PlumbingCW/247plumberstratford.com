Water line repair in Stratford, CT, from meter to house.

The water service line is the buried pipe between the water meter and your home. Repair means finding the leak or failure along that run and replacing the damaged section or the whole line, often with minimal digging when the path allows it.

If water is bubbling up in the yard, your house has lost pressure or the meter will not stop, call the 24/7 line. Call to ask about timing. Close the main shutoff inside the house to limit the loss, and tell the dispatcher where the water is surfacing.

Act now.

When a service line leak is an emergency.

A broken service line can flood a yard and basement wall, undermine a driveway and run up a water bill in days. Sudden loss of pressure or no water at all can also come from a failed line.

Close the main valve inside the house. If water still flows past it, the leak is between the street and your shutoff, and the water utility's curb stop may need to be closed. Do not dig by the leak before an underground utility locate, because gas, electric and communication lines may sit nearby.

Warning signs.

Signs of a service line problem.

A wet or sunken patch in the yard.

Water escaping from a buried pipe saturates soil and can erode the ground above it.

Falling water pressure.

A leak between meter and house starves every fixture, and the drop is noticeable at the highest taps.

A spinning meter with everything off.

Flow with no use means water is leaving the system somewhere.

A sudden rise in the water bill.

A buried leak can run for weeks before it shows on the surface.

Muddy or discolored water.

A broken line can draw soil in when pressure drops.

Hissing near the foundation.

A leaking line can be heard where it enters the house.

Low water pressure: line problem or something else.

Low pressure at every fixture points to the service line, the main shutoff valve or a pressure reducing valve that has failed. Low pressure at one fixture is usually a clogged aerator or cartridge, which our faucet repair page covers.

We test pressure with a gauge at an outdoor hose bib. A reading well below your usual level, or one that swings, tells us where to look. A pressure reducing valve that has failed can also cause high pressure, which strains every pipe in the house, so we test for that, too.

Local note.

Service lines and lead in Stratford.

Aquarion supplies water to Stratford through its Eastern Fairfield County system. Its 2025 water quality report says that homes built before 1986 may have lead service lines, though most were installed in homes built before 1930, and that Aquarion's goal is to eliminate all lead service lines across its systems.

Customers can check the material of their service line through the lead service line inventory at aquarionwater.com/lead. If you are replacing your side of the line, ask Aquarion how its side is handled so the job is coordinated and the connection is not left in a mixed-material state.

The national 811 number connects you to the utility locating service, and a locate request before digging is the standard first step for any buried line work.
