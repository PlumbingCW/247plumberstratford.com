Repair or Replace a Water Heater in Stratford, CT: A Decision Guide

When the shower runs cold, the choice is between fixing the old tank and installing a new one. A few clear signs tell you which way to lean.

Written by the Stratford 24/7 Plumbing team.
Talk to a plumber.
(203) 720-6269.
Call now

Start with age, then look for leaks.

Most tank water heaters last roughly eight to twelve years, depending on water quality, maintenance and use. The date on the data plate tells you how old yours is. If the tank is past its tenth year and has a problem, replacement deserves a serious look, because the next failure may not be far behind.

Leaks change the question entirely. A leak from a fitting or valve on the top is often repairable. Water seeping from the tank body is not, since the steel liner has failed. If you see water around the base, turn off the power or gas and shut the cold water supply to the tank, then call a plumber.

Warning signs.

Signs a water heater is near the end.

Rust colored hot water.

Corrosion inside the tank sheds rust, and replacing the anode rod only helps if the tank is still sound.

Rumbling or popping.

Sediment layers heat unevenly and make noise.

Water around the base.

Even small drips can signal a failing tank.

Lukewarm water that runs out fast.

A failed element or heavy sediment cuts capacity.

Rising energy bills.

A tank that works harder to heat water costs more to run.

Age past ten years.

Even if it still works, the risk of failure climbs.

The case for repair.

Repair is the right call when the problem is a part and the tank is otherwise healthy. Thermostats, heating elements, igniters, thermocouples, gas valves and relief valves are all replaceable. If your tank is younger and the repair is modest, it can give you years of service.

Ask the plumber what failed and whether other parts are close behind. A tank that needs a gas valve and a new anode rod in the same year is often telling you something about its overall condition.

The case for replacement, including tankless.

Replacement makes sense when the tank has failed or repairs are piling up. A new tank brings a fresh warranty and better efficiency. A tankless unit saves floor space and provides hot water on demand, though it costs more up front and needs proper venting and gas sizing. Our tankless water heater page covers that choice.

Capacity matters too. If your family has grown or you added a bathroom, a larger tank or a second unit may fit better than a like-for-like swap.

Capacity, efficiency and fuel.

A replacement is also a chance to fix what never worked. If a four person household ran out of hot water every morning with a small tank, a larger tank or a different type of heater solves the underlying problem instead of repeating it. Efficiency ratings on newer units can lower operating costs compared with older tanks.

Fuel matters as well. Switching between gas and electric changes the venting, wiring or gas piping required, so it is a larger project than a like-for-like swap. Ask the plumber to walk through what the change would involve before you commit.

Local note.

Stratford note: soft water and tank life.

Aquarion's 2025 water quality report for the Eastern Fairfield County system, which serves Stratford, lists average hardness at about 3 grains per gallon. Soft water means less mineral scale on heating elements and tank bottoms than in hard water areas, so age and corrosion usually decide a tank's life here.

Ask the Stratford building department whether your replacement needs a permit, and note that neighbors in Trumbull and Shelton should ask their own towns.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission urges users to lower their water heaters to 120 degrees Fahrenheit to reduce the risk of tap water scalds, so ask the plumber to set the thermostat with that guideline in mind.
