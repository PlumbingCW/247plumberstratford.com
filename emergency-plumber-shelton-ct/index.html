Winter, thaw and summer storms in Shelton.

Shelton rises from the Housatonic into wooded hills, and the plumbing there feels the seasons more than shoreline towns do. Pipes in garages, crawlspaces and exterior walls freeze first, and the break often shows up when the ice melts. Our 24/7 line is open all night.

Spring brings a second wave. Melting snow and heavy rain raise groundwater on slopes, so basements and sump pits take on water after a long dry stretch ends.

Act now.

Frozen or burst pipe in Shelton: act in this order.

If water has slowed to a trickle in a hard freeze, open the faucet and warm the pipe with a hair dryer on low. Never use an open flame. If you see a split or water is spraying, close the main valve first.

Then call the 24/7 line. Tell the dispatcher where the pipe runs, whether the wall or ceiling is wet and whether anyone is without heat.

Ledge and rock.

Rocky ground can mean shallow pipe runs that are more exposed to frost.

Before we arrive in Shelton.

A few quick steps can keep a thaw from becoming a flood.

Step 1.

Close the main valve.

Turn it clockwise and open the lowest tap to drain the lines.

Step 2.

Do not use flame.

Never thaw a pipe with a torch. A hair dryer on low is safer.

Step 3.

Keep heat on.

Open cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls and keep the thermostat up.

Step 4.

Call the 24/7 line.

Water and roads in Shelton.

Aquarion serves Shelton as part of its Eastern Fairfield County system, which also supplies Stratford. If water stops on your whole street at once, the problem may be in the main, so check with neighbors and Aquarion before assuming your own line has failed.

Route 8 and Route 110 are the usual ways in from Stratford, and winter conditions can add time, so the dispatcher gives a realistic window. Call 811 before any digging for a line repair.
