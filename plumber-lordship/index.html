Neighborhood guide.

Where Lordship sits and why it matters for plumbing.

Lordship is a small neighborhood in the south of Stratford. It stands on a peninsula that was once an island and was extended with fill into Long Island Sound. Sikorsky Memorial Airport lies to the north and separates it from the rest of town, and only two roads reach it, both parts of Route 113. The 2020 census counted 2,954 residents and 1,462 housing units.

Real estate listings show Colonials, Capes and ranches, along with condominium communities such as Breakwater Key and Short Beach Village. We cannot tell you the age or piping of any particular house from a map. A good plumber looks at what is behind your walls before choosing a repair.

Act now.

Basement water or a gurgling floor drain in Lordship.

Shut off power to the wet area only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Turn off the main water valve if a pipe is leaking, and keep children and pets away from standing water. If the water is coming up through a floor drain, stop using sinks, tubs and washers so you do not add to it.

Then call. A plumber can check whether the cause is a failed sump pump, a blocked line or a leak, and explain the options before work starts.

Before storm season in Lordship.

A few minutes of testing is cheaper than cleaning up a flooded basement. Our basement waterproofing page explains permanent fixes.

Step 1.

Test the sump pump.

Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float lift and the pump discharge.

Step 2.

Check the discharge.

Make sure the pipe outside is clear and drains away from the house.

Step 3.

Lift valuables.

Move boxes and electronics off the basement floor and onto shelves.

Step 4.

Find the main shutoff.

Know where it is and make sure it turns before you need it.

Corrosion near the water.

Check whether neighbors have it too, then have the pressure regulator and shutoff valves tested.

Local note.

Water, sewer and digging in Lordship.

Stratford water comes from Aquarion's Eastern Fairfield County system, which its 2025 report lists at about 3 grains per gallon of hardness. Whether your street uses the town sewer or septic is something we cannot confirm from here, so ask Stratford Water Pollution Control about your address.

Before any digging, including a new sump discharge line, call 811 so underground utilities are marked. Ask the Stratford building department which permits apply.
