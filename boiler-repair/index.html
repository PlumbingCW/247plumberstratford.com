Boiler repair in Stratford, CT, when the heat quits.

A boiler heats water or makes steam and sends it through pipes to radiators or baseboard heaters. Repair means finding the failed part in the burner, circulator pump, controls, valves or expansion tank and restoring safe, steady heat.

In cold weather, call the 24/7 line the moment the heat stops. Call to ask about timing. Check that the thermostat is on and calling for heat, the switch at the boiler is on and the gas valve is open, but do not repeatedly reset a boiler that locks out.

Act now.

Boiler emergency: what to do first.

Treat no heat in freezing weather as an emergency, since pipes can freeze when a house cools. Treat any smell of gas, soot around the boiler, water leaking from the unit or a hissing relief valve as an emergency too.

If you smell gas, leave the house, call 911 and the gas utility from outside, and do not turn any switch on or off. If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, go outside into fresh air and call 911. Once the home is safe, call us for the boiler.

Symptoms.

Common boiler problems.

No heat, boiler off.

A tripped limit, failed ignition, closed gas valve or lockout can stop the burner.

Radiators cold at the top.

Trapped air in a hot water system keeps heated water from filling the radiator and may need bleeding.

Banging or kettling noises.

Scale or sediment on the heat exchanger, or low water flow, can cause banging.

Pressure dropping or rising.

A leak, a failed expansion tank or an autofill fault can move the gauge out of range.

Water around the base.

A leaking fitting, a failed relief valve or a cracked unit calls for immediate attention.

Pump noise or no circulation.

A failing circulator pump leaves rooms cold even though the boiler runs.

Reading the pressure gauge.

Most residential hot water boilers sit at roughly 12 to 15 psi when cold and climb a few pounds when hot, though your boiler's manual sets the right range. A gauge that is near zero suggests a leak or a failed autofill. One that climbs high suggests a faulty expansion tank or overfilled system.

The relief valve opens to prevent dangerous pressure and has a discharge pipe pointing downward. If it is dripping or blowing, do not cap it. Call us so the cause is found, since the valve is a safety device and not the problem.

Repair or replace the boiler.

If hot water comes from an indirect tank tied to the boiler, a failure in either can look like the other. Our water heater repair page explains how that side is checked.

Local note.

Boilers in Stratford homes.

Many Connecticut homes heat with a boiler and baseboard or radiators, and a boiler is often tucked into a basement or utility room next to the water heater. If your boiler room is the same space as the sump or the laundry, water problems and heating problems can overlap.

Before winter, ask a plumber to check the pressure gauge, relief valve and circulator. A service call in October costs less than an emergency visit in January, and a carbon monoxide alarm near the boiler gives an extra margin of safety.

According to the International Code Council, Connecticut's state building codes are based on the 2021 International Codes, so ask the Stratford building department which edition applies to your project. According to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, you can check whether a contractor holds an active license through the state eLicense lookup, which is worth doing before any plumber starts work.
