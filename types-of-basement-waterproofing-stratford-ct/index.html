Types of Basement Waterproofing for Stratford, CT Homes

Basement waterproofing is not one product. It is a set of methods, and the right one depends on where the water comes from and how much of it there is.

Written by the Stratford 24/7 Plumbing team.

(203) 720-6269.
Call now

Start with the source of the water.

Basement water has a few common sources. Surface water runs toward the house from gutters and sloping soil. Groundwater rises and presses on walls and the floor. Plumbing leaks and high humidity can also make a basement feel wet. Treating the wrong source wastes money, so the first step is to find out which one you have.

A plumber or waterproofing contractor can look at water marks, cracks, the grading outside and the sump pit if one exists. Rule out a plumbing leak early with leak detection, since a leaking pipe is a simple fix compared with a drainage system.

Which fix.

Matching the method to the problem.

Water after heavy rain only.

Check gutters, downspouts and grading before anything else.

A crack that leaks.

Injection can seal a stable crack, but not a moving one.

Water at the floor and wall joint.

An interior drain and sump pump handle this well.

Damp walls with no visible water.

Humidity control and sealing may be enough.

Water through a block wall.

Interior drains relieve pressure behind block, and exterior work is more thorough.

Rising groundwater.

A sump with a backup protects against high water tables.

Interior and exterior systems.

An interior drain system sits along the inside edge of the footing under the slab and carries water to a sump pit. It is less invasive than digging outside, and it works with a sump pump that discharges away from the house.

Exterior waterproofing excavates down to the footing, cleans and seals the wall, adds drainage and backfills. It is the most thorough fix for water entering through walls, but it costs more and disrupts landscaping, decks and walkways. It is usually reserved for severe or recurring problems.

How to approach the decision.

1.

Document the water.

Note when it appears, where it shows up and how high it gets.

2.

Fix the cheap causes first.

Gutters, downspouts and grading cost the least and help every system.

3.

Get an inspection.

A plumber checks for leaks and evaluates the drainage options.

4.

Choose the least invasive workable fix.

Match the method to the source and the severity.

Interior or exterior waterproofing.

Neither is always the answer. The right choice depends on your foundation and your water source.

Interior drain.

  • Less digging and disruption.
  • Works with a sump pump.
  • Relieves pressure under the slab.
  • Does not stop water entering the wall.

Exterior waterproofing.

  • Stops water at the outside wall.
  • Most complete fix.
  • Requires excavation.
  • Usually costs more.

Maintenance that keeps any system working.

Test the sump pump every season by filling the pit with water and watching it cycle. Clean gutters in spring and fall. Check that downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation, and look at the soil grade around the house after storms. A system that is tested and kept clear lasts far longer than one that is ignored.

If you finish a basement, leave access to the pump and cleanouts. Hiding the pit behind a wall makes service difficult when it is needed most.

Humidity and mold: what to do first.

A basement can feel damp without water coming in. Warm, humid air meets cool concrete and condenses. A dehumidifier, good air circulation and sealed ducts reduce moisture. If you see mold, fix the moisture first, then clean the affected area or call a professional for larger patches.

Do not store cardboard boxes directly on a basement floor. Plastic bins on shelves keep belongings dry and make it easier to spot a new leak.

Local note.

Stratford note: shoreline, river and permits.

Stratford lies along Long Island Sound with the Housatonic River on its eastern edge. In shoreline neighborhoods, high groundwater and storm surge can make basement water a recurring problem. Call 811 before digging, and ask the Stratford building department whether permits apply.

Coastal homes in Milford and Fairfield face the same conditions and often need a pump and backup power in addition to drains.

The national 811 number connects you to the utility locating service, and a locate request before digging is the standard first step for any buried line work.

What waterproofing does not do.

It does not remove mold that is already growing, and it does not fix a plumbing leak. It also does not make an unfinished basement habitable by itself. Finishing a basement needs proper insulation, ventilation and moisture control as well.

Be wary of any contractor who promises a dry basement forever without inspecting your house. A good contractor explains what the system does, where it can fail and what maintenance it needs, such as testing the pump each season.
