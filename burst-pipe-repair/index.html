Burst pipe repair in Stratford, CT, starting with the shutoff.

A burst pipe is a split or break in a water supply line, usually caused by freezing, corrosion or a failed joint. Repair means cutting out the damaged section and replacing it with sound pipe, or in some cases replacing a longer run.

The 24/7 line is open all night. Close the main water shutoff valve right now, open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the pipes, switch off power to wet areas if it is safe, and move belongings away from the water.

Act now.

When a burst pipe is an emergency: your first ten minutes.

Every minute counts. A half-inch pipe can release many gallons in a short time, and a split in a ceiling line will soak insulation and drywall below it. Find the main shutoff, usually near where the water line enters the house or at the water meter, and turn it fully clockwise.

Turn off the water heater power or gas if the heater might drain empty, and do not use any electrical item standing in water. Then call us and tell the dispatcher where the water is, whether the main is off and whether any ceiling has started to sag.

Before the plumber arrives.

These four steps limit damage while help is on the way.

Step 1.

Step 2.

Open the lowest faucet and flush toilets once to relieve pressure.

Step 3.

Keep everyone away from wet electrical areas and switch off the breaker if safe.

Step 4.

Document the damage.

Take photos and video before you clean up for the insurance claim.

Frozen pipes in Connecticut winters.

Pipes freeze when cold air reaches them, and the usual places are exterior walls, unheated garages, crawlspaces and attics. The ice itself does not always break the pipe. The break often comes when ice melts and pressure surges through a weak point.

If water slows to a trickle in cold weather, open the faucet, warm the pipe gently with a hair dryer on a low setting and never use an open flame. If a pipe is already split or you cannot find the frozen section, shut the main and call us. After thawing, we inspect for splits that will show up only once water is flowing again.

Common pipe materials and how they fail.

If several leaks have shown up in the same house, repair becomes a repeat expense, and our repiping page explains when a whole-house replacement saves money.

Pipe.Typical failure.Usual repair.
Copper.Pinhole leaks from corrosion, or splits after freezing.Cut out the section and sweat or press in new copper.
Galvanized steel.Rust from the inside, low pressure, leaks at threads.Replace the run, often a sign of wider aging.
PEX.Splits at fittings, damage from freezing or chewed lines.Replace the section with crimp or expansion fittings.
CPVC.Cracks from age, heat or over-tightened joints.Replace the section and check nearby joints.

Burst pipes and Stratford's winters.

Stratford is on the coast of Long Island Sound, but winter cold still reaches exposed lines in garages, crawlspaces and unheated porches. Homes with additions or garages built over the years can have supply lines running through the coldest space.
