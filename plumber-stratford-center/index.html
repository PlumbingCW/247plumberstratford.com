Historic district.

A historic center with documented older buildings.

The Stratford Center Historic District has been on the National Register of Historic Places since 1983. Within it, the Academy Hill Historic District is a local district established in 1987 and expanded in 1996. It surrounds Academy Hill Green, about six acres on the slopes of Watch House Hill.

The district includes Elm Street, Main Street, East Broadway, Judson Place, White Street and Broad Street. Its buildings date from the 17th to the 20th centuries and include Federal, Greek Revival, Italianate, Gothic, Queen Anne and Colonial Revival styles.

A building that has stood through several centuries has usually been altered more than once. We do not claim any particular pipe material for any address. The honest way to know is to look, with a camera in the drain or a small opening in the wall.

Before you plan.

Historic district review and exterior work.

The Academy Hill district is overseen by a Historic District Commission. If your plan changes something visible from outside, such as a vent termination, a new exterior shutoff or a relocated meter, ask the Commission and the Stratford building department whether approval is needed before work begins.

We cannot say whether a given interior job needs review, and a plumber cannot decide that for you. Asking first costs a phone call and can avoid a stop-work problem.

Lead lines and water in the center.

Aquarion publishes a lead service line inventory at aquarionwater.com/lead. In an older part of town, check it before assuming anything about the line that brings water into your building. Call 811 before any digging near the street or the yard.
