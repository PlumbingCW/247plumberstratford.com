Sewer line repair in Stratford, CT.

Sewer line repair fixes a damaged section of the pipe that carries wastewater from your house to the town main or a septic tank. Damage comes from roots, shifting soil, corrosion, a belly that holds water, or a joint that has separated underground.

If sewage is backing up, call the 24/7 line now. Call to ask about timing. Stop running water, keep everyone off wet flooring, and do not use chemical drain cleaners, which can burn the person who clears the pipe next.

Act now.

Sewer backup: what to do first.

A sewer backup is a health problem as well as a plumbing problem. Shut off the water to the house only if you can reach the main valve safely, and never wade through standing water if appliances or outlets are near it.

Open windows if the odor is strong, move people and pets away, and tell the dispatcher what is backing up. A shower, toilet or basement floor drain that overflows first tells us roughly where the line is blocked.

Warning signs.

Signs your sewer line needs repair, not just a cleaning.

Repeated backups.

A line that backs up more than once or twice a year after cleaning usually has a structural cause.

Sinkholes or soft spots in the yard.

Escaping wastewater can wash soil away and leave a dip over the pipe.

Sewer smell outdoors.

Gas rising through the lawn or near the foundation points to a break or open joint.

Slow drains throughout the house.

When every fixture slows down, the blockage or damage is below the branches.

Lush green patches in the lawn.

A leaking line fertilizes the grass above it, a clue worth reporting to the plumber.

Rodents or insects entering from the drains.

A cracked or open pipe gives pests a path into the house.

Repair, lining or replacement: the honest trade-offs.

Pipe lining places a resin-soaked tube inside the old pipe, which hardens into a new pipe within a pipe. A common industry standard for this method is ASTM F1216. We will tell you plainly if lining will not work for your pipe, and our sewer camera inspection shows exactly what condition the pipe is in.

Option.Best for.Trade-off.
Spot repair.One cracked or offset section found by camera.Fixes the short run only, the rest of the pipe stays as it was.
Cured-in-place lining.A sound but cracked or rooty pipe with a clear path.Needs a clean pipe first and cannot fix a fully collapsed section.

Open trench replacement.Collapsed, offset or bellied pipe.Most disruptive, but gives a brand-new line with correct slope.

Cast iron and older pipe materials.

Older homes often have cast iron drain lines inside and clay or cast iron lines under the yard. Cast iron rusts from the inside, forming rough scale that catches debris and eventually thins the wall. Clay joints can open and let roots in.

Repair options for cast iron include replacing a failed section with modern plastic pipe or lining the line. A camera inspection decides which makes sense. We will not recommend digging up a pipe that lining or a spot repair could save.

Local note.

Sewer lines in Stratford yards.

Before any digging in Stratford, call 811, the national Call Before You Dig number, so underground utilities are marked. Then ask the town's Water Pollution Control office where the private lateral ends and the town main begins, because who repairs what depends on that line.

Septic tank pumping is a separate service. If your home is on septic rather than the town sewer, tell the dispatcher so the right contractor is sent, and we will say so if septic work is what your problem needs.

The national 811 number connects you to the utility locating service, and a locate request before digging is the standard first step for any buried line work. According to the International Code Council, Connecticut's state building codes are based on the 2021 International Codes, so ask the Stratford building department which edition applies to your project.
